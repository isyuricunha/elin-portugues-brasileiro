This week, I bring to you Yowyn's wisdom, gathered over generations of farmers! Isn't that exciting? Every adventurer should appreciate good food, and that starts with growing crops! Sow the seeds, water them, watch them grow — that isn't wrong, but there's more to it!

{p}
<b>Watering, Harvesting and Fertilizer</b>

I know adventurers are busy travelling all over North Tyris; you can't be home every day and worry your plants will get thirsty. It's okay: watering your crops is ideal, but they won't die if you don't and will just grow slower. That's actually better if you go on a long journey, as you don't want produce to rot in the field while you're away.

It's also important to note that a planting will only produce one crop! Visitors to Yowyn are surprised when fruit trees or crops like tomatoes are cleared after harvest — they believe those plants will refruit! Silly city-slickers who think food comes from the market, ehe~
{p}
Fertilizer often confuses novice farmers because its effect isn't immediately obvious. It gives the plant a little growth boost and helps it ripen faster, by about 10%. The effect isn't retroactive and doesn't stack, so fertilize early!

{p}
<b>Crop Improvement and Strains</b>

As you get better at farming, your crops will respond to your love and care, becoming more nutritious, filling, and worth more at market! They might also develop quirks we call "strains": you might discover that your imos are starting to taste spicy, or the tomatoes are becoming really crisp!

Me? I like rich collagen in all my plants — it's really good for my skin! Once a strain's appeared in a seed line, it's there for good! You can change it by mixing the seeds with alchemical concoctions like mercury and blood of fallen angel in a wooden mortar, but those are really rare!
{p}
Improving your plants takes time and skill. Sickling plants yourself helps you pick out the best seeds in each crop, making for much faster improvement compared to delegating the task!

Forest and grassland nefia are richly fertile, and skilled adventurers have spotted patches of high-quality crops growing in the wild! If you want to save some time improving a new crop, why not seek out some good seeds to start with?

Remember, crops can only get so tasty, but even afterwards they'll keep on selling for more and get more filling! Just one of my neighbor's apples can <i>really</i> pad out a pie!

{p}
<b>Trees and Hydroponics</b>

Trees take longer to grow: fruit trees require 40 days, others 30 days compared to the 20 days of most crops. More importantly, mature trees planted on tilled soil will always yield seeds when cut down. You don't need to sickle half your fruit trees to replant, which means the yield works out to that of other crops! Twice the time, but also double the crop — isn't that amazing?

Their long growth time does mean you have to be careful near winter if you don't have any sun lamps. Some city-slickers think trees won't mind being left out over winter. Would <i>you</i> be okay with being left out in the cold? Of course not! Bring in the seeds before you lose them!
{p}
Hydroponics is usually used for grains like wheat and rice, the seeds being directly planted in water. Cultivation of deep seaweed on land is similar: three pours from a bottomless pot will create the necessary water depth.

Crops suited for hydroponics are considered permanently watered. Other plants will still grow, but gain no benefit!

Farming is one of those skills that's easy to pick up, but hard to master. I hope that people won't forget the natural world, wherever you live!
