Adventurer's Weekly No. 23: Slimes,Aqui Teola Correspondent Dr. Mossman,10,1

<i>Welcome to Adventurer's Weekly, where we cover topics near and dear to your brave hearts.</i>


From Aqui Teola, I bring you the latest news in science and technology: this week, I'm covering research into one of Ylva's most overlooked creatures: the slime.

Slimes are common pests across Ylva: these gelatinous blobs dwell in damp places, are often dangerously acidic, and multiply rapidly if unchecked. The wild cousin of the domesticated putit, slimes are regarded by scholars as the most basic of creatures.
{p}
But it's this primordial nature that grants slimes their versatility: over time, slimes can adapt to almost any environment, consume any organic matter as food, and absorb genetic material to exhibit myriad abilities. For three years now, Aqui Teola has studied slimes to harness the medical potential of this versatility, and I'm sure we're close to a breakthrough!

A creature so common, yet so amazing — let's explore the world of the slime!


<b>Predatory Evolution</b>

This is a slime's defining trait, although "predatory" is somewhat misleading; while a slime can absorb dropped genes from its prey, it isn't limited to just that.
{p}
In fact, a slime "devouring" genetic material from another living creature can be quite affectionate and doesn't harm the "victim", although the unsettling sensations often result in the slime being attacked anyway! Some people do find it rather pleasurable, though. The more practised a slime is at "devouring", the stronger the genetic material obtained.

Anyways! Whether it be from a consumed gene or "devouring", once this assimilation is complete, the slime flawlessly mimics the feats, abilities, and even body parts encoded in the gene's memories. A wellspring of infinite possibilities; its primordial body reshapes itself in the blink of an eye to adapt to any adversity!
{p}
Perhaps the only reason slimes are not more dominant in Ylva's ecology is that their bodies can only ingest so many genes at once; a slime attempting to exceed its limit will lose the oldest gene it's absorbed. Conversely, consuming an inferior gene purges the most recent gene, and consuming a brain cell shuffles the slime's entire genetic structure! Isn't that amazing?

As slimes grow, the number of genes they can hold at once gradually increases, allowing for some rather frightful elder slimes that've devoured genes from a variety of creatures. Adventurers should approach these with caution!

{p}
<b>Versatility</b>

A slime's power lies in its adaptability, its primordial biology combining genes from different creatures in ways that would normally be impossible, making the whole more than the sum of its parts. It's not confined to one role, changing forms on a whim so long as genetic material is available, leading to its widespread proliferation across Ylva.

This "predatory evolution" is likely how today's putit was domesticated from wild slimes, and then rapidly sub-divided into a wide number of morphologies: the beth putit, metal putit, forest putit and water putit for example, not to mention the many colors of snow putit much beloved by us all.
{p}
By studying the humble slime, it's our hope that we'll be able to improve gene engineering methods. In the meantime, adventurers shouldn't underestimate slimes!
